ATHENS BARS & NIGHTLIFE

The difference between market entry and tourism is whether the people, suppliers and neighbourhood knowledge are already inside the operation when something goes wrong.

Starting in a new city

The Athens work began with the advantage of previous nightlife operating experience, but no city can simply be copied onto another. The local rhythm is different: neighbourhoods behave differently, suppliers have different strengths, music scenes overlap in different ways and the audience has its own tolerance for what feels imported.

So the work became local quickly. Bar concepts, builds and programming had to connect room, drinks, music, staff and calendar to the way Athens actually moves.

The Dubel Team record describes the Athens scope as bar concept and build, licensing and suppliers, music programming, staff and training, and local-network access.

What we actually owned

A bar is a chain of small operating decisions that only becomes visible when one of them fails. The concept affects the fit-out. The fit-out affects service flow. The drinks affect training and speed. Programming affects who arrives and when. Staffing affects whether the experience survives a busy night.

That is why the job cannot be separated cleanly into “creative” and “operational.” The creative idea is only useful if the room can execute it repeatedly. The calendar is part of the product. The staff are part of the product. The supplier network is part of the product.

What made it hard

New markets create an information problem. Public information can tell you what exists, but not always what is changing, who is reliable or why one street feels different from the next. The solution was not more desk research. It was relationships with people already inside the city’s hospitality and cultural life.

What stayed with us

This became the foundation of Dubel Team’s Greece market-entry work. A local network is not a contact list. It is a way to reduce bad assumptions. The useful local answer is often not “who can do this?” but “who can actually do this well, now, in this neighbourhood, under these constraints?”
