/06 The questions everyone asks

BEFORE YOU ask.

I don't know what I need. Is that a problem?

It's the normal starting point, and it's most of the value. Very few people arrive with a spec — they arrive with a week that's too full and a feeling that something should be easier. Working out what to build is step one, and it's free.

What does it cost?

Depends entirely on scope, so any number quoted before the conversation would be a guess dressed as a price. What is fixed: you get one written price before anything is built, and it doesn't move unless you change what you asked for. Small builds for independents start small on purpose.

Who owns it when it's done?

You do. The code, the domains, the accounts, the data — all in your name, handed over in writing. No hostage situation, no platform you can only leave by starting again.

How long does it take?

A focused site or tool is usually two to four weeks. A full product with accounts, payments and an admin side is closer to six to eight. If a deadline is real — a season, an opening, an event — say so on the first call and we scope backwards from it.

I already have a website and some systems. Do we start over?

Usually not. Replacing everything is the expensive answer and often the wrong one. More often we keep what earns its place, fix the two things costing you money, and connect the pieces that should have been talking all along.

What happens if you disappear?

Fair question to ask anyone who builds you something. Everything runs on standard, boring, widely-used technology, documented in plain language, in accounts you own. Any competent developer can pick it up. Nothing here is designed to make you dependent.

Why a company that also does hospitality and events?

Because the software that fails is almost never the software that was coded badly — it's the software built by someone who has never run the floor it's meant to serve. Fifteen years of owning a P&L is the part most developers can't offer.
