It was a dangerous spark. He put it out quickly, the way you pinch out a match between your fingers, and focused on the equations he knew how to solve: how much food was in the house. How many days the water would last. Matan and a few others were at Nova, at the party!&quot;</p><p data-i="23">She drew a breath and went on in another voice, lower: &quot;And look what Omer Hermesh is writing from Kfar Aza. It didn't come for anyone.</p><p data-i="31">She gripped her rifle in shaking hands and gave orders that no longer meant much. They fell back to the small room at the rear of the war room. Roni kept reporting into her microphone, sentence after sentence, the way they had been taught. Her voice didn't shake.</p><p data-i="32">Minutes stretched into hours. Outside, gunfire gave way to silence and then to gunfire again, and someone kept trying the door.</p><p data-i="33">Then the war room filled with smoke.</p><p data-i="34">Something was thrown in, and fire spread. The smoke was thick and black, and within seconds they couldn't see anything. Only the red lights of dead screens, coughing, hands feeling for a wall.</p><p data-i="35">&quot;Roni!&quot; Natalie shouted. &quot;Roni, give me your hand!&quot;</p><p data-i="36">Someone pulled her by the arm. A back door. Air. She tumbled out with a few others, onto her knees, lungs burning, and turned around.</p><p data-i="37">Roni didn't come out after her.</p><p data-i="38">She waited. One second. Another. Smoke kept pouring from the doorway, and the gunfire came closer. Someone dragged her on.</p><p data-i="39">&quot;I'm sorry,&quot; she whispered, not knowing to whom. &quot;I'm sorry, Roni.&quot;</p><h2 class="scene" data-i="40">Midday · Gaza</h2><p data-i="41">At midday the streets of Gaza were full of crowds. Some of them were celebrating. Jeeps and pick-ups came back from the fence carrying people who had been abducted – soldiers and civilians, young and old – and sometimes bodies. The crowd gathered round them, shouting, filming.</p><p data-i="42">Mehran saw it only on his phone screen. A young woman in pyjamas, barefoot, led between armed men. The face of a child. He switched off the screen and laid the phone on the floor, face down.</p><p data-i="43">&quot;What have we done,&quot; he whispered.
