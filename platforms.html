OFFsides

Onside or off? You have one call.

One real moment a day. One judgment. You against the VAR room — and against everyone who swore, out loud, in a bar, that they read the game better than the officials do. Thirty seconds to play, all day to argue about.

Football has never really been about the score. It's the shouting, the replays, the friend who is confidently wrong every single week. Offsides takes the best argument in the sport and hands it to you daily, in your pocket, with a streak on the line.

Discovery · Athens
takemeout.dubelteam.com ↗

TAKEMeOUT!

Your Athens, perfectly planned.

You land in Athens with two days and forty open tabs. TakeMeOut! closes them: three complete plans in about thirty seconds, first coffee to last call, each one an actual sequence rather than a list of pins scattered across a map.

What separates it from every other city guide is where the taste comes from. Ratings tell you a place is popular. They don't tell you it's dead on a Tuesday, that the good room is upstairs, or that the street behind it has quietly become the reason to go. That kind of knowledge sits with people who live there — and that is the part of Dubel Team the app is built on.

DUbid

A little neighbourhood, a lot of football.

Fantasy football for the Israeli league, with one rule that changes the whole game: you pick exactly one player from every club. No stacking the two teams everyone likes — you have to have an opinion about all of them. The captain scores triple, and that single choice decides most rounds.

It runs on live league data across cyclical rounds — squads, fixtures, scoring, standings — which is the unglamorous part that has to be right every single week or the product is worthless. Hebrew end to end, built right-to-left from the first line rather than translated into it.

retzach.dubelteam.com ↗

RETzach · The Archive

Everything the episode covered, in one place.

A listeners' archive for the Hebrew true-crime podcast hosted by Maya Gazit and Shai Magal: evidence, victims, letters, timelines and sources, organised case by case so that what you heard in an episode can be read, checked and followed properly.

The discipline is the point. Files appear only once they have been documented by hand against verified sources — which is why some cases sit marked "coming soon" rather than half-built. Research, content and editorial belong to the podcast; Dubel Team built and maintains the archive around them.

Public wall · Anonymous
fuckyou.dubelteam.com ↗

FUCK You

Say it. Don't send it.

Everyone has a message they are better off not sending. This is where it goes instead: a name, a reason, an intensity, posted anonymously with no account and no tracking — and then everyone else gets to say me too and push it up the wall.

It is a joke with real engineering under it. Duplicate detection so one person doesn't get four entries, names accepted in any script, ranking and search across the whole wall, a moderation and admin layer, and a tone held carefully on the right side of the line: comedy, not an indictment.

The honest version

WHO'S THE team?

Fair question. There is no floor of desks behind this.

Dubel Team is run by one operator. What stands behind it is a network — people who know their world from the inside. Hospitality operators and chefs. Bartenders and DJs. Artists, designers, promoters, producers. People who actually live in the cities we work in and can tell you what changed last month.

They are not employees. They are not a roster we rent out. They are the reason a brief comes back with a real answer instead of a search result.

We don't know everything.
We know people who do.

Sometimes the best information is online. Sometimes it is sitting at the bar next to someone who has been running the room for ten years. The network is the asset — the work is how we use it.
