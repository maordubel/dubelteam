Field Note 03 · Football · Mostar · By Maor Harel

HŠK ZRINJSKI MOSTAR

A football club can look healthy from the stands while still carrying friction behind the scenes. Organizational development starts where the crowd cannot see it.

The brief

The work with HŠK Zrinjski Mostar focused on organizational development: how departments fit together, where commercial value sits, how fan culture connects to the institution, and what needs to be aligned internally before the club tries to grow outward.

Football makes structural weaknesses easy to postpone because the match keeps arriving. There is always another fixture, another travel day, another commercial opportunity and another immediate decision. The danger is allowing the weekly rhythm to substitute for an operating model.

The Dubel Team work record lists club structure, commercial strategy, fan culture and advisory as the core areas of the Zrinjski engagement.

What we looked at

The useful questions were basic on purpose. Who owns which decision? Where does information move slowly? Which commercial assets are being treated as isolated tasks instead of part of one system? Where does the club’s identity create value that the organization is not yet translating into products, partnerships or supporter experience?

That kind of work is less visible than a campaign, but it is often more durable. A club cannot consistently sell sponsorship, improve supporter experience or build new revenue if responsibility is unclear and every initiative depends on a small number of people remembering how things were done last time.

What made it hard

Clubs are emotional institutions. Structural changes that make sense on a spreadsheet still have to fit the rhythm, hierarchy and identity of the organization. The job is not to import a generic model from another market. It is to understand where the club already works well and build from there.

What stayed with us

The lesson was simple: growth is an organizational question before it is a marketing question. If the internal structure is unclear, more attention only creates more pressure. If the structure works, commercial and cultural opportunities become easier to execute because the club knows who can actually carry them.
